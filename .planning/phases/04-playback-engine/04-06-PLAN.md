<objective>
**As an** operator watching the cell, **I want to** see the robot parked on the
right of the rail for printing jobs and on the left for milling jobs, **so that**
the seventh axis has a visible reason to exist instead of always sitting dead
centre.

Gap closure for **G-04-1** (04-UAT.md, test 1, `missing` item 3). The user reported:
"the rail carriage doesn't move in any g-code sample so it's not checkable if it
will work… Make the robot start from the right side of the rig and left side for
milling. In this way the rig rail has a motive for being there."

The diagnosis in the gap record is exact: `resolveRailPosition` works and is tested,
but Phase 2's D-06 anchor step X-centres **every** toolpath onto `RAIL_CENTER_X`, so
the resolver always lands back at centre. Nothing is broken — the rail is simply
never given off-centre work to reach.

**Deliberate deviation from the gap record's suggested fix, to record in the
summary.** The gap's `missing` item proposes threading `cellMode` "into
`compileTrajectory`/`resolveRailPosition`". This plan threads it one layer earlier
instead — into the *anchor* the toolpath is placed at — and leaves `compile.ts` and
`rail.ts`'s resolver algorithm completely untouched. The rail then moves because the
**work** is off-centre, which is the physically honest reason a rail axis moves at
all, and it reuses the existing, already-tested resolution heuristic rather than
adding a mode parameter that would let the resolved station disagree with where the
job actually sits. Because the whole scene shifts by one rigid X translation, every
reach, singularity and table-clearance property Phase 3 established is preserved by
construction — and that is asserted directly, not assumed.

Purpose: this is the last of the four G-04-1 items, and it is the one the user
called out as making the rig's central mechanism meaningful.

Output: a per-mode rail station shared by the anchor and the workbench, a parser
that accepts an explicit anchor, a store that resolves the mode at selection time,
and a blocking human verification covering all three gap-closure plans together.
</objective>

data, no new user-supplied input surface. No package-manager installs occur in this
plan, so the package-legitimacy gate does not apply.
