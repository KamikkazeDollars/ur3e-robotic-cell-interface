## Confirmed root cause — verified, not guessed

Both screenshots were cropped and upscaled during planning (`Photo 1.png` 1445x835,
`Photo 2.png` 1505x893 — **different window sizes, so they are NOT a controlled A/B;
camera framing differs between them**). The zoomed carriage/rail junction is decisive.

### The defect: the end-stop block is swallowed by the carriage at every travel extreme

`RailRig.tsx` places both end-stop meshes at `RAIL_TRAVEL.min` / `RAIL_TRAVEL.max` —
the exact X coordinates the carriage's **centre** reaches. It should place them where
the carriage's **face** stops. With `CARRIAGE_BASE_WIDTH = 0.34` and
`END_STOP_WIDTH = 0.07`, at `railPos = RAIL_TRAVEL.min = -1.095`:

| Element | X-span at the min travel limit |
|---|---|
| carriage base plate | `[-1.265, -0.925]` |
| end-stop block | `[-1.130, -1.060]` |

The end-stop is **100% inside** the carriage. It is not merely hidden: `END_STOP_DEPTH`
(0.34) exceeds `CARRIAGE_BLOCK_DEPTH` (0.22), and `END_STOP_HEIGHT` reaches Y 0.16 while
the base plate stops at Y 0.09 — so the stop's front face protrudes through the carriage's
mounting block between Y 0.09 and Y 0.16 as a bare 0.07 m square.

**That square is visible in `Photo 1.png`.** Measured against the carriage base plate as
a 0.34 m ruler in the upscaled crop, it is ~0.077 m wide — `END_STOP_WIDTH` within
measurement error. In `Photo 2.png` (railPos -0.885, carriage span `[-1.055, -0.715]`,
end-stop span `[-1.130, -1.060]`, a 0.005 m gap) that same block stands clear and reads
correctly as the rail's terminus. **The end-stop being swallowed vs. standing proud is
precisely the difference between the two photos.**

### Why 0.305 m of "margin" still reads as near-total absence of rail

The user's demanded question, answered with numbers:

1. **0.305 m is measured from the carriage's centre, not its face.** Real visible track
   past the carriage's outer face is `0.305 - CARRIAGE_BASE_WIDTH/2` = **0.135 m** —
   40% of the carriage's own width.
2. **The one element that visually terminates the rail is invisible** (defect above). A
   bare 0.135 m stub with nothing capping it does not read as "rail"; it reads as a
   fragment.
3. **The near rail bar's stub is self-occluded.** `CARRIAGE_BASE_DEPTH` is 0.38 m vs the
   rails' outer Z envelope of 0.27 m, so the base plate overhangs the near bar by 0.055 m
   in +Z while standing 0.05 m above rail top — from any low camera angle the plate hides
   the near bar, leaving only a sliver of the far bar.

Margin was never the whole story, which is exactly why four rounds of resizing failed.

### Why restoring ±1.3 against the current fixed track would reproduce round 3 verbatim

`TRACK_HALF_SPAN_M` is the fixed literal `1.4`. At `RAIL_TRAVEL = ±1.3` the carriage's
outer face lands at `1.3 + 0.17 = 1.47` — **0.07 m past the rail's own end at 1.4.** The
carriage literally hangs off the track into empty floor. That is the exact state quick
260816-s4e shipped and the user rejected. A fixed track-span literal cannot be correct
for any travel range by construction; it must be derived.

### Hypotheses actively ruled out (do not re-open)

- **Coordinate/transform bug — RULED OUT.** `cellStore.manualJog.railPos` ->
  `CellScene.tsx` selector -> `<RailRig railPos>` -> `<group position={[railPos,0,0]}>`,
  with `RobotModel` a scene-graph *child* of that group, so the robot cannot decouple
  from the carriage. Screen-space cross-check on `Photo 1.png`: robot base (world
  -1.095) at x≈956 px, carriage left face (world -1.265) at x≈888 px, rail left end
  (world -1.4) at x≈840 px — implied scale 400 and 356 px/m respectively, consistent
  under perspective foreshortening. **The rendered positions match the math exactly.**
- **Lighting / shadow-frustum / material falloff — RULED OUT.** The two photos are
  0.21 m apart in world X and the same `SCENE_PALETTE.rail.hex` material renders brightly
  in one; no distance falloff acts over 0.21 m. `directionalLight`'s default shadow camera
  is a ±5 m orthographic frustum, comfortably covering the whole track.
- **Base-plate / rail-top coplanarity — RULED OUT as the visible artifact.**
  `CARRIAGE_BASE_CENTER_Y` does put the plate's bottom face at exactly Y 0.04, coplanar
  with both rail tops. But three.js default `FrontSide` culling means the plate's
  downward face and the rails' upward faces are never both drawn, so this does not
  z-fight. **Do not "fix" this** — `CARRIAGE_TOP_Y` feeds `toolpath-anchor.ts`'s
  `WORKBENCH_TOP_Y` derivation and moving it would ripple into the toolpath anchor.
</root_cause>
